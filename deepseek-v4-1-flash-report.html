New low-cost AI model: same daily work, one-twentieth the price?

A new AI model (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, released 10 Sept 2026) claims to match the best expensive models at a fraction of the cost. We checked the claim. Short answer: for routine coding and automation work, yes. For complex, long, or security-sensitive work, no.

Bottom line

The answer in 30 seconds

This model handles everyday AI coding and automation jobs about as well as models costing 17–40x more, and it works fast. But overall it does not match Opus 5: the independent lab scores Opus 5 at 54 vs 40 for this model. It falls behind on long complex projects, security work, and hard expert questions. Suggestion: use it for daily bulk work to cut costs; keep the premium model for everything important.

Money

What would we save?

AI is billed by volume of text read and written. This model's list price is $0.30 per million units read and $1.20 per million written (half that in off-peak hours). The premium alternatives charge roughly $4–5 to read and $20–30 to write. That is where the 17–40x gap comes from.

A concrete example: a typical AI coding session (reads ~2M units, writes ~200K) costs about $0.42 on this model vs $1.72 on DeepSeek's own older model — and roughly $8–15 on a premium model. For teams running agents daily, the monthly difference is large.

One catch on cost: this model is chatty — it writes nearly twice as many words as average to finish a job. Since writing is the expensive half, real savings are big but slightly smaller than the headline prices suggest.
