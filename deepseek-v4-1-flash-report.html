New low-cost AI model: same daily work, one-twentieth the price?

A new AI model (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, released 10 Sept 2026) claims to match the best expensive models at a fraction of the cost. We re-checked the claim against DeepSeek's own docs, DeepSeek's published model card, Artificial Analysis's independent lab results, and live social posts — and corrected several numbers that didn't hold up on re-verification. Short answer stands: for routine coding and automation work, yes. For complex, long, or security-sensitive work, no.

Bottom line

The answer in 30 seconds

This model handles everyday AI coding and automation jobs about as well as models costing 17–40x more, and it works fast. But overall it does not match Claude Opus 5: the independent lab (Artificial Analysis) scores Opus 5 at 51 vs 40 for this model in their own head-to-head comparison. It falls behind on long complex projects, writing whole programs unsupervised, and hard expert questions. Suggestion: use it for daily bulk work to cut costs; keep the premium model for everything important.

Money

What would we save?

AI is billed by volume of text read and written. This model's confirmed list price is $0.30 per million tokens read and $1.20 per million written (half that, $0.15/$0.60, in off-peak hours: 1–4am and 6–10am UTC on weekdays are "peak"; everything else is off-peak). Claude Opus 5 charges $5 to read and $25 to write per million tokens; GPT-6 Astra charges $10 to read and $50 to write. That produces the 17x (vs Opus, output-weighted) to 41x (vs Astra, output-weighted) gap.

On a blended basis — the mix of cached/input/output tokens a real agent workload actually uses — Artificial Analysis puts this model at $0.18 per million blended tokens vs $3.85 for Opus 5, a ~21x gap, and $0.27 per completed benchmark task vs $4.21–$5.86 for Opus 5 (the two Artificial Analysis pages we checked show slightly different Opus cost figures because they used two different versions of the same benchmark suite — flagged in the log below).

Versus DeepSeek's own outgoing model (V4 Flash 0731): Artificial Analysis states V4.1 Flash is about 20% cheaper on a blended basis than its predecessor. We could not independently reproduce a clean "cost per coding session" figure for the old model — DeepSeek changed 0731's list price more than once during its life, and third-party trackers disagree on which snapshot to use — so we're not repeating a specific dollar figure for that comparison.

One catch on cost: this model is chatty — DeepSeek's own model card and Artificial Analysis both flag it as one of the most verbose models measured (it used more output tokens per task than even Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol in Artificial Analysis's test). Since writing is the expensive half of the bill, real savings are big but somewhat smaller than the headline per-token prices alone suggest.

What are people actually saying?

Beyond the company's own scores, here's what independent users report. Linked, in their own words, shortened only for length. We re-opened every link below directly to confirm it's live and says what's quoted.

+

"Back on top of the open-source leaderboard and extremely cheap." — Thomas Wolf, co-founder of Hugging Face (X, 61K views). The most-shared endorsement so far.

+

"$0.30 built me a 7-page client website in 15 minutes — 80% what the client wanted." — Reddit user Odd-Duty-7205 (full thread). Honest caveat from the same user: it was a simple site and he guided it throughout, giving content and a design reference link.

+

"Goes straight to the main problem, fixes it, reports back — better than [rival] for focused coding." — Reddit user Artium99, in the same thread, after a day of use. Adds that a rival model "over-engineers and goes around in circles." Another user in the thread, Santzes, says it could "replace 85-90% of my big lab model usage."

+

Independent lab confirms the value: Artificial Analysis (X, 269K views) measured cost of $0.27 per task — ~7x below same-tier rivals GLM-5.3 and Kimi K3, first place (tied with GPT-6 Astra) on an office-automation test, and long-document reasoning equal to GPT-5.6 Sol and Gemini 3.8 Flash.

−

"I feel like the model is constantly on amphetamine — extremely fast but it just cannot focus… the problem-solving goes into 10 directions at once." — Reddit user Mawrak, same thread. A separate tester on X (BridgeMind, 125K views) clocked it at 344 tokens/sec yet finished slower than two rival models on a fixed benchmark task because it burns so many tokens: "even though it runs so fast it ends up completing tasks very slowly."

−

"BTW it is clearly experimental and currently the model is under heavy load…" — X user Ivan Fioravanti, describing early file-editing failures during launch-day testing. A separate Redditor (wwa56) summed up the size/quality tradeoff bluntly: only "20% better for 100% increase in size" versus the prior generation.

The pattern: users love the speed and price for everyday coding; complaints are about focus (too much output, too scattered) and launch-week reliability — not about the core quality on narrow coding tasks.

Risks

What could go wrong?

1

It is 2 days old. Early testers (Ivan Fioravanti, above) report it struggling under load and failing some file edits on launch day; others report it losing focus mid-task. Expect fixes over the coming weeks — we have not seen a credible report of it switching languages mid-answer specific to this model, but launch-week bugs of some kind are near-certain for any 2-day-old release.

2

Not for unsupervised critical work. Anything long, complex, or security-sensitive should stay on the premium model for now — the benchmark gaps on those specific categories are large and consistent, not marginal.

3

Check the bill. DeepSeek's own pricing page confirms it: the company originally said all "V4 Pro" traffic would auto-migrate to the new cheap Flash pricing on Sept 14, then reversed that — a footnote on the live pricing page now says V4 Pro API access continues past Sept 14 "with the billing method remaining unchanged." Verify which model and which price tier your account is actually being billed under before scaling up.

Suggestion

What we recommend

  • Move routine AI coding and automation to this model now — same or near-same quality on daily work, ~17-40x lower per-token cost depending on comparator, faster results.
  • Keep the premium model for the hard minority — long unsupervised projects, fresh whole-program builds, and expert-level questions, where the gap to Opus 5 is large and consistent across independent and vendor benchmarks alike.
  • Review after one month — by then independent testers will have confirmed (or corrected) the early numbers, and launch-week bugs should be fixed.
