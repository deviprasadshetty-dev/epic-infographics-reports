Madhva vs Shankara: 7 claims, checked so simply a 10-year-old gets it

Madhvacharya (13th century) disagreed strongly with Adi Shankaracharya (8th century). Later followers on both sides added sharper charges — including about the Prapanchasara Tantra being vulgar or black magic. This page checks each charge one by one: what is actually said, what the evidence shows, and what is only opinion.

00 — First, who are they?

Two teachers, one library, opposite conclusions

Think of the Vedas and Upanishads as one big library. Shankara reads it and says: at the deepest level, you (jiva) and the Ultimate (Brahman) are one — like wave and ocean. That is Advaita, non-duality. Madhva reads the same library and says: no, God (Vishnu) and you are forever different — like child and father. That is Dvaita, duality. Both love the same books. They just answer the biggest question differently: are we one with God, or always separate?

  • Shankara's main books: commentaries on Brahma Sutras, 10 Upanishads, Bhagavad Gita + short manual Upadesha-Sahasri. Everyone agrees these are his.
  • Madhva's main books: Brahma Sutra Bhashya, Anuvyakhyana, Mayavada-Khandana, Vishnu-Tattva-Vinirnaya, Tantra-Sara-Sangraha (his own temple-ritual manual).
  • Rule for this page: philosophy disagreement = fair debate. Personal insult or invented verse = must be checked with evidence.

Claim 01

Claim 1Philosophical opinion, not fact

"Advaita is prachchannam bauddham — hidden Buddhism. Mayavada is a false shastra."

Kid version: Madhva says Shankara's idea — "the world is maya, only Brahman is real" — sounds exactly like Buddhists who say "the world is empty." So he calls it Buddhism wearing a Hindu dress.
Evidence for the chargeMadhva's Mayavada-Khandana argues identity of jiva-Brahman is self-contradictory. He quotes the famous line mayavadam asat-shastram prachchannam bauddham uchyate — Mayavada is a false doctrine, disguised Buddhism — a verse found in Padma Purana / quoted in Gauḍiya texts and Madhva polemics.
What checking showsSimilarity is real: both use "unreal world" language. But Shankara explicitly refutes Buddhism in his Brahma Sutra Bhashya (2.2), accepts Veda + Atman + Brahman + moksha, which Buddhism rejects. Modern scholars call this a family resemblance, not identity. Calling it "Buddhism" is a debate label, not a lab test.
Verdict reasoning: Fair as philosophy ("your logic collapses into X"), unfair as history ("Shankara was a secret Buddhist"). No evidence Shankara followed Buddha; he debated Buddhists and was later praised for bringing people back to Vedic fold.

Claim 02

Claim 2Misleading

"Shankara twisted Tat Tvam Asi. Madhva corrects it as Atat Tvam Asi / Sa Atma Atat Tvam Asi."

Kid version: The Upanishad says "Tat Tvam Asi — You are That." Shankara reads it plainly: you ARE Brahman. Madhva splits the words differently with grammar rules: "You are NOT That." Both say "my grammar is right."
Evidence for the chargeMadhva and followers use Panini grammar (sa-atma-atat-tvam-asi) to argue the sentence can mean difference, supported by context where father teaches son as separate. Advaita readers admit the sentence needs interpretation (jahad-ajahal-lakshana).
What checking showsNo manuscript of Chandogya Upanishad reads "atat." All printed editions read "tat tvam asi." Madhva's reading is a re-division, not a discovered text. Shankara did not "fake" the words; he interpreted the plain words literally. You may prefer either interpretation, but "Shankara fabricated scripture" is false — the words are there in every edition.

Claim 03

Claim 3False as fact

"Advaitins are deceitful demons / asuras. Shankara was a demon."

Kid version: Some stories say bad demons were reborn as philosophers to confuse people. Some speakers point at Shankara and say "that is him." That is name-calling, not proof.
Where it comes fromMadhvavijaya ( biography of Madhva) and later haridasa lore describe opponents as asuric; online summaries claim Madhva called Advaitins "deceitful demons." Polemical verses about "kutarka" opponents get stretched into personal demonization.
What checking showsMadhva's formal works debate logic, not birth certificates. No pramana (perception, inference, scripture) can prove "X soul is a demon." Advaita tradition equally calls Dvaita "dualist delusion." Both are sectarian insults. History records Shankara as a Vedic sannyasi who debated Mandana Mishra peacefully before a woman judge (Ubhaya Bharati) with flower garlands — not demonic conduct.
Bottom line for kids: If someone loses a debate, calling the other side "demon" does not win it. Ignore the insult, examine the argument.

Claim 04

Claim 4Unproven — faith story

"Shiva incarnated as Shankara on Vishnu's order to deliberately spread a false doctrine in Kali Yuga."

Kid version: A Purana story says: God told Shiva, "Go down dressed as a priest and teach 'all is one' to test people." Believers quote it as CCTV footage of heaven. But stories are not CCTV.
Evidence citedPadma Purana Uttara 236.7: mayavadam asac-chastram prachchannam bauddham ucyate / mayaiva vihitam devi kalau brahmana-murtina — "O Devi, in Kali age I myself will teach Mayavada in brahmana form." Advaita lists also cite it discussing authenticity debates.
What checking showsPuranas contain opposite stories praising Shankara as Shiva's grace restoring Veda. You cannot use one sect's Purana as court evidence against another sect — Shaivas, Vaishnavas, Shaktas each have "God says we are right" verses. Historians treat these as post-facto theology, not biography. As faith, respect it; as fact, unproven.

Claim 05 — Prapanchasara

Claim 5False — verse imported

"In Prapanchasara, Shankara teaches applying menstrual blood to the body — disgusting and vulgar."

Kid version: Imagine a cookbook for rice. Someone says "this rice book tells you to eat mud!" You open the rice book — no mud recipe. They actually took the mud recipe from a different book about clay pots and pasted it on the rice book. That is what happened here.
What Prapanchasara actually contains33 core chapters: creation, speech-letters, Kundalini, diksha-homa, Sarasvati, Tripura, Bhuvaneshvari, Lakshmi, Durga, Ganesha, Krishna, Pranava-yoga, long Vishnu section, Shiva, metres, yantras, guru-worship + ethics. Cited by Raghava Bhatta on Sharada-tilaka as authoritative Mantra-shastra. Avalon edition on Archive.org shows dhyana-mantras-yantras, Dakshinachara, not smearing rites.
Where blood verses really areYoni Tantra ("the yoni which has bled is suitable for worship"), Kaulajnananirnaya 8 (semen + menstrual blood + ghee as pure substances), Matrikabheda 8 (Svayambhu/Kunda/Gola classification), Kaulavali-nirnaya 17 (Woodroffe summary: body is made of semen-blood, purity is of mind). Those Kaula texts treat rajas as sacred, not dirty. Critics lift those lines and file them under "Prapanchasara." No chapter-verse from Avalon's Prapanchasara shows the alleged smearing injunction.
Is Tantra ever sexual? Yes, some Kaula rites use yoni-puja and sexual yoga symbolically and sometimes literally — but only for initiated adults, in guarded context, treating fluid as shakti. Calling that "vulgar porn" is like calling surgery "stabbing." Insider = sacred; outsider reading literally = shock.

Claim 06 — Prapanchasara

Claim 6Half-true, label false

"Prapanchasara teaches vashikarana and black magic."

Kid version: A knife can cut vegetables or hurt someone. The manual lists "knife can cut X, Y, Z." That does not make the author a thief. Mantra manuals list ten uses — peace, health, children, control, confuse, freeze, chase, kill. Same knife, ten jobs. Misuse is the user's fault.
True partYes, like all mantra texts, Prapanchasara gives prayoga-phala: same mantra with different procedure gives shantika, paushtika, vashikarana, mohana, stambhana, vidveshana, uchchatana, marana. Avalon himself recounts a Nepal pandit refusing to let Shatkarma mantras be printed, fearing misuse punishable by law.
False label"Black magic" implies secret evil cult. In shastra these are statecraft/defense + pastoral help: keep king allied (vashya), freeze infiltrators (stambhana), confuse enemy propaganda (mohana), chase occupier (uchchatana), kill terrorist (marana). Text demands purascharana + siddhi + guru + prayoga knowledge (never fully in books) and ends with guru-worship + ethical rules. Atharva Veda has the same list — we don't call Veda black magic.

Claim 07

Claim 7Misleading

"Shankara contradicts himself — Brahma Sutra vs Tantra — so Prapanchasara must be fake. And nobody writes 400 books in 32 years."

Kid version: A maths teacher writes a PhD paper AND a rhymes book for babies. Different style is not lying — different students need different books. And "Shankaracharya" is also a title, like "Principal" — many principals signed papers, not one man.
Fair concernWestern test: compare everything to Brahma Sutra Bhashya; if style/philosophy differs, mark doubtful. 400+ attributions in 32 years is genuinely suspicious. Even traditional Advaitins admit doubt about Prapanchasara. Colophon "Shankaracharya" could be any pontiff.
Why it doesn't prove fakeBhashya = for advanced monks; Tantra manual = for temple worshippers needing murti-mantra-yantra. Different genre, different audience — contradiction is expected, not proof of fraud. Tradition never claimed one man hand-wrote all 400 alone — disciples compiled, later acharyas added. Modern scholars lean "school attribution / pseudo-Shankara," which means reverent compilation, not forgery. Honest position: unproven either way.
